Planning summary

Actionable guidance for GB OOH planning from Route 59 · Week in March 2026 · 10s:0s schedule · GB adults 15+

The headline case for OOH
OOH reaches 53.9M GB adults every week — 97% of the population. The 13.9Bn weekly impacts figure reflects an incredibly high frequency of exposure across 374,552 frames nationally. No other single medium achieves this level of unduplicated reach in a week. OOH is not a niche amplifier; it is a mass reach channel.
Reach is remarkably consistent across demographics. Cover ranges only from 95.9% (C2DE) to 97.7% (ABC1) — a 1.8pp spread. This means OOH cannot be used to selectively avoid or over-index on broad demographic groups. For audience precision, environment and geography selection are the primary levers.
Environment selection guide
Environment is your most powerful targeting tool. Each of the 13 measured environments has a distinct reach profile, digital/classic split, and audience dwell context. Start here before thinking about regions or media owners.
Roadside (Inventory with a vehicular audience) 62% classic
5.6Bn weekly impacts · 95.72% cover · 53.2M reach
This plan targets Roadside inventory with a vehicular audience and delivers impacts across digital and classic formats, with a larger share allocated to classic units — prioritize classic placements to maximize mass reach. Coverage is high (95.72%), so use this for broad-awareness campaigns aimed at drivers and reserve the digital portion for time-sensitive or contextually relevant messaging.
Underground — London 75% classic
3.2Bn weekly impacts · 6.62% cover · 3.7M reach
Reach and cover shown are the combined GB Underground total (London + Glasgow), not city-specific.
Disproportionately high impacts relative to reach means very high average frequency — this is a captive, repeat-exposure environment rather than a broad-reach one. Predominantly classic (75%) with a meaningful digital share (25%). High dwell time; strong for brand-building and premium creative formats.
Shopping centres (interior) 93% digital
1.9Bn weekly impacts · 10.71% cover · 6.0M reach
Shopping centres (interior) are heavily weighted to digital and deliver very high weekly impacts, so they work well to build repeated in-venue exposure. Coverage is limited (10.71%), so use this plan to drive frequency among shopping-centre visitors and pair it with broader-reach channels to lift overall market coverage. Proximity to point of purchase makes this the standout environment for retail and FMCG briefs. Dynamic content, dayparting and contextual triggers are all possible. Strong against Main Shoppers.
Rail Station 54% digital
1.3Bn weekly impacts · 10.73% cover · 6.0M reach
Rail Station environment where digital formats make up the majority of impacts. Coverage is 10.73%, so use this inventory to drive repeat commuter exposures—this suits frequency-focused campaigns (brand reminders, promotions targeting regular commuters) rather than single-shot reach buys. Prioritize digital creative rotation and concentrated flighting to maximize repeat touchpoints rather than aiming for broad unique reach.
Bus Exterior 100% classic
1.1Bn weekly impacts · 88.05% cover · 48.9M reach
Second only to roadside for national reach. Entirely classic (no digital). Moves through residential areas roadside misses — effective for local and regional coverage. Strong value proposition on CPM. All inventory is with Global.
Supermarket (exterior) 78% digital
212M weekly impacts · 25.64% cover · 14.3M reach
This supermarket exterior inventory is digital-dominant (78% of weekly impacts). It delivers broad weekly scale and covers about a quarter of the population, so use it when you need mass reach or broad-funnel coverage. Prioritise digital budget to capture the majority of impressions and, if you require a mixed-media presence, set aside a portion of spend for the smaller classic component, which contributes under one-quarter of impacts. Strongly over-indexes for Main Shoppers, and relevant for grocery, FMCG, and health brands.
Tube carriage interior 100% classic
210M weekly impacts · 5.94% cover · 3.3M reach
Tube carriage interior delivers concentrated in-journey exposure and is entirely classic with no digital. It drives high weekly impacts and strong frequency but covers a relatively small share of the population (5.94%). Use it for frequency-driven creative and campaigns that rely on repeated impressions in carriages. If you need broader market coverage, supplement with higher-cover formats.
Taxi (Taxi exteriors) 100% classic
150M weekly impacts · 22.25% cover · 12.4M reach
Entirely classic (no digital inventory in this dataset). Reach is driven by taxi riders and pedestrians seeing exterior ads. Urban-skewed, good for premium or London-centric brands. Ubiquitous is the dominant media owner for this environment.
Motorway service stations 66% digital
83M weekly impacts · 3.92% cover · 2.2M reach
This plan targets Motorway service stations and is digital-weighted, favoring dynamic creative and repeat exposure across service sites. Use it for campaigns prioritising tactical, high-frequency messaging rather than broad market coverage; because overall cover is modest (3.92%), layer with wider OOH or additional schedules to materially expand reach. Smaller in reach but a highly specific audience — people making long-distance journeys. Good for automotive, travel, and food/drink brands. Captive dwell audience with purchase intent (they stop to shop).
Roadside (Inventory with a vehicular audience) (40%), Underground — London (23%), Shopping centres (interior) (14%) account for roughly 77% of total weekly impacts. These three environments deliver strong scale, covering 77% of measured inventory. Use them when you need broad reach: prioritize Roadside and Underground — London for the bulk of coverage and add Shopping centres (interior) for incremental exposure.
Geography: where the weight is
London dominates: the capital generates 8.4Bn of 13.9Bn total GB weekly impacts — 61% of all OOH weight from one region. Any national plan will be heavily London-indexed by default: London accounts for 61% of all GB impacts. If London is not a priority, actively weight down or explicitly exclude London inventory.

Classic vs digital: the trade-off
Digital frames are only 5% of total inventory (20,214 of 374,552 frames) but generate a disproportionate share of impacts due to location — shopping centres, rail, underground, motorway services, and London roadside all skew heavily digital. Digital screens are concentrated where footfall is highest.

Media owner landscape
The market is highly concentrated. The top four media owners — Global (229,059 frames), KBH (59,394 frames), JCDecaux (28,594 frames), Bauer Media Outdoor (28,200 frames) — account for 92% of all measured frames. A plan built around these four will achieve near-total GB coverage; they account for 92% of frames, so prioritize them for mass-reach campaigns and add smaller owners only to plug specific regional or environment gaps.

Key caveats for planning
Schedule sensitivity: The 13.9Bn weekly impacts figure is based on a 10s spot / 0s break schedule. A more realistic 10s:50s schedule reduces total impacts by approximately 35% to ~9.0Bn. Always confirm the commercial schedule when presenting impact figures to clients or comparing across sources.
Data week: All Route 59 figures come from a single week in March 2026. Route releases quarterly. Reach figures are stable year-round (53.8M–53.9M). Impact figures may vary by season — outdoor pedestrian footfall is higher in summer, shopping centre indoor traffic is higher in winter.
Reach is not additive: Environment cover figures cannot be summed. The 95.72% Roadside (Inventory with a vehicular audience) cover and 88.05% Bus (Bus exterior) cover do not add to 184%. Unduplicated total cover across all environments is 96.93%. Use the total figure for cross-environment planning.
London dominance in impacts vs reach: London generates 61% of all impacts but only 28% of reach (14.9M of 53.9M). This means London plans generate very high frequency. If frequency is not the objective, London-heavy plans may be inefficient compared to a broader national spread.
Digital inventory is scarce outside London: Only 5% of frames are digital nationally, and they are heavily concentrated in London and major retail/transit environments. Digital-only plans outside London will face availability constraints. Confirm inventory before committing to a digital-heavy specification in regional markets.
Media owner concentration: Global alone owns 61% of all measured frames. Any large-scale national plan will require Global as a primary partner. Global holds 61% share — include KBH, JCDecaux and Bauer Media Outdoor to reduce reliance on a single owner. Use this mix when you need supplier diversification and stronger negotiation leverage across vendors.
