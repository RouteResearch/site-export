Planning summary

Actionable guidance for GB OOH planning from Route 58 · Week in March 2025 · 10s:0s schedule · GB adults 15+

The headline case for OOH
OOH reaches 53.9M GB adults every week — 97% of the population. No other single medium achieves this level of unduplicated reach in a week. The 13.98Bn weekly impacts figure reflects an incredibly high frequency of exposure across 383,908 frames nationally. OOH is not a niche amplifier; it is a mass reach channel.
Reach is remarkably consistent across demographics. Cover ranges only from 96.0% (C2DE) to 97.8% (ABC1) — a 1.8pp spread. This means OOH cannot be used to selectively avoid or over-index on broad demographic groups. For audience precision, environment and geography selection are the primary levers.
Environment selection guide
Environment is your most powerful targeting tool. Each of the 13 measured environments has a distinct reach profile, digital/classic split, and audience dwell context. Start here before thinking about regions or media owners.
Roadside 63% classic
5.6Bn weekly impacts · 95.95% cover · 53.3M reach
The backbone of any national OOH plan. Near-universal reach with national distribution. Use for mass coverage; supplement with other environments for frequency and dwell. Majority classic, with a meaningful digital share (37%).
Underground — London 75% classic
3.2Bn weekly impacts · 6.62% cover · 3.7M reach
Disproportionately high impacts relative to reach — 3.2Bn impacts from just 3.7M people means very high frequency. London-only by definition. Predominantly classic, with a meaningful digital share (25%). High-dwell environment; strong for brand messaging. Premium CPM, but unmatched for London saturation.
Shopping Centre Interior 93% digital
2.0Bn weekly impacts · 10.71% cover · 6.0M reach
Almost entirely digital inventory. Proximity to point of purchase makes this the standout environment for retail and FMCG briefs. Dynamic content, dayparting and contextual triggers are all possible. Strong against Main Shoppers.
Rail Station 53% digital
1.3Bn weekly impacts · 10.73% cover · 6.0M reach
Commuter audience with dwell time. Strong for financial services, tech, and anything targeting workers. Almost 50/50 classic/digital split gives flexibility. JCDecaux and Global are dominant here.
Bus Exterior 100% classic
1.1Bn weekly impacts · 88.05% cover · 48.9M reach
Second only to roadside for national reach at 88%. Entirely classic (no digital). Moves through residential areas roadside misses — effective for local and regional coverage. Strong value proposition on CPM. All inventory is with Global (Ubiquitous).
Supermarket Exterior 78% digital
212M weekly impacts · 25.66% cover · 14.3M reach
Strongly over-indexes for Main Shoppers. High digital proportion enables real-time promotions, price messaging and contextual triggers. Reach of 25% is solid for a targeted environment. Relevant for grocery, FMCG, and health brands.
Tube Carriage Interior 100% classic
210M weekly impacts · 5.94% cover · 3.3M reach
London-only, entirely classic (no digital inventory in this dataset), very high dwell (average Tube journey ~15 min). Captive audience with no phone signal is ideal for static, longer-dwell creative. High frequency per person. Use to complement roadside when London is a key geography.
Taxi 100% classic
150M weekly impacts · 22.25% cover · 12.4M reach
Entirely classic (no digital inventory in this dataset). The 22% reach is driven by taxi riders and pedestrians seeing exterior ads. Urban-skewed, good for premium or London-centric brands. KBH is the dominant media owner for this environment.
Motorway Service Station 66% digital
84M weekly impacts · 3.91% cover · 2.2M reach
Smaller in reach but a highly specific audience — people making long-distance journeys. Majority digital inventory. Good for automotive, travel, and food/drink brands. Captive dwell audience with purchase intent (they stop to shop).
Three environments account for roughly 77% of total weekly impacts: Roadside (40%), Underground London (23%), and Shopping Centre Interior (14%). Any plan that includes these three has strong scale. Add Bus and Rail Station for near-universal coverage with different audience contexts.
Geography: where the weight is
London dominates: the capital generates 8.4Bn of 14.0Bn total GB weekly impacts — 60% of all OOH weight from one region. Any national plan will be heavily London-indexed by default. If London is not a priority, you need to actively weight down or explicitly exclude London inventory.

South & South-East is the most under-served region relative to population: 9.9M reach but only 539M impacts — a fraction of what London generates from a similar-sized audience. Deliberately weighting S&SE can be efficient if your audience is there. The Midlands also has a large population (9.3M reach) relative to its impact weight.
Digital share doesn't simply track London. London itself is only 37% digital — similar to South & South-East (37%) and lower than the Midlands (52%), North East (53%), and Central Scotland (49%). Wales (34%) has the lowest digital share nationally. Don't assume a London/non-London split for digital availability — check the region-level figures above before committing to a digital-heavy regional spec.
Classic vs digital: the trade-off
Digital frames are only 5% of total inventory (19,936 of 383,908 frames) but generate a disproportionate share of impacts due to location — shopping centres, rail, underground, motorway services, and London roadside all skew heavily digital. Digital screens are concentrated where footfall is highest.

Media owner landscape
The market is highly concentrated. The top four media owners — Global (230,059 frames), KBH (67,248), JCDecaux (28,684), and Bauer Media Outdoor (28,297) — account for 92% of all measured frames. A plan built around these four will achieve near-total GB coverage.

Key caveats for planning
Schedule sensitivity: The 13.98Bn weekly impacts figure is based on a 10s spot / 0s break schedule. A more realistic 10s:50s schedule reduces total impacts by approximately 35% to ~9.1Bn. Always confirm the commercial schedule when presenting impact figures to clients or comparing across sources.
Data week: All Route 58 figures come from a single week in March 2025. Route releases quarterly. Reach figures are stable year-round (53.8M–53.9M). Impact figures may vary by season — outdoor pedestrian footfall is higher in summer, shopping centre indoor traffic is higher in winter.
Reach is not additive: Environment cover figures cannot be summed. The 95.95% roadside cover and 88.05% bus cover do not add to 184%. Unduplicated total cover across all environments is 96.97%. Use the total figure for cross-environment planning.
London dominance in impacts vs reach: London generates 60% of all impacts but only 28% of reach (14.96M of 53.9M). This means London plans generate very high frequency. If frequency is not the objective, London-heavy plans may be inefficient compared to a broader national spread.
Digital inventory is scarce outside London: Only 5% of frames are digital nationally, and they are heavily concentrated in London and major retail/transit environments. Digital-only plans outside London will face availability constraints. Confirm inventory before committing to a digital-heavy specification in regional markets.
Media owner concentration: Global alone owns 60% of all measured frames. Any large-scale national plan will require Global as a primary partner. This creates negotiation leverage considerations — a diversified plan using JCDecaux and Bauer Media Outdoor alongside Global will give more competitive pressure on pricing.
